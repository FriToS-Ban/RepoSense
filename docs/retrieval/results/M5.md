## What was built

- **Personalized PageRank by local push** (Andersen, Chung & Lang 2006, forward push). The restart distribution is uniform over the seeds. A seed added by the change has no node in the base graph, so its share goes to its HEAD-side dependencies from the overlay. A node whose residual is at least ε × degree keeps α of it and passes the rest to its neighbours in proportion to the transition weights. Cost is O(1/(εα)) pushes, independent of graph size, and hubs are damped by construction.
- **Typed, direction-aware transitions:** callee 1.0, caller 0.9, overridden / override 0.8, tested / tests 0.5, method → class 0.4, class → method 0.2, each multiplied by the calibrated edge confidence. Module nodes are not traversed, because an import edge would put every importer of a package within two hops.
- **Typed BFS** to depth 2 is the ablation (ADR-06). A candidate's score is the best product of edge weights along a path.
- **Two ways to use them:**
  - `multihop="ppr" | "bfs"` appends up to 40 multi-hop candidates *after* E's plan-weighted 1-hop list (systems `E_ppr`, `E_bfs`);
  - the `ppr` channel ranks every candidate by PPR mass alone (system `P`).

  Each is also fused with the lexical channel by RRF (`EA_ppr`, `EA_bfs`, `PA`).
- **Tuning protocol.** The T1 queries are split 50/50 by a hash of the query id (`eval.stats.split_of`, salt `split-v1`; 178 dev, 159 test), and `eval.compare --split` restricts a comparison to one half. α is chosen on T1-dev only. T2 is reported for every α for transparency, but nothing is selected on it.

Tests (7, `backend/tests/test_ppr.py`):
- local push matches power iteration to 1e-6
- ordering and two-hop reach
- tolerance vs work
- BFS relation paths
- exclusion of seeds and modules, and `min_hops`
- restart from an added seed's dependencies
- the pipeline's multi-hop tail and `ppr` channel

41 backend tests pass.

## T1 (circular; read as a pipeline check)

deps `2hop_only` view (140 queries with second-hop judgments; ground truth = the references of the references, i.e. callee chains):

| System | R@budget | R@10 | nDCG@10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| E (1-hop, M3) | .042 | .022 | .011 |
| E_ppr | .624 | .247 | .100 |
| **E_bfs** | **.676** | **.354** | **.151** |
| P | .622 | .280 | .116 |
| EA | .147 | .049 | .023 |
| EA_ppr | .432 | .082 | .045 |
| EA_bfs | .602 | .148 | .077 |

- **Both methods deliver second-hop context.** Against E, both gain at p_holm 4e-4: E_ppr +.582 R@budget, E_bfs +.634.
- **On T1, BFS beats PPR** (R@budget −.052, p_holm .042; R@10 −.108, p_holm .002). That is expected, because T1's second hop is exactly the callee-of-callee path that typed BFS scores highest (1.0 × 1.0).
- **Hop-1 R@10 "gains" are a measurement artifact.** E_ppr also raises deps `sym_xfile` R@10 (.854 → .972) although its first 1-hop ranks are unchanged. The cause: with more candidates, packing upgrades fewer items to full source (4.1 → 3.5 per query). A class shown in full inserts all its methods into the ranked list (it covers them), which pushes later items down. Fewer full classes therefore move judged items up without any change in ranking. Rank-based metrics on packed contexts should treat a full class's methods as tied at the class's rank. That is a metric change for every system, so it is deferred to the next re-baseline and recorded here.

## Findings

1. **Multi-hop raises the graph's ceiling on real changes, and PPR is the right method for it.** Pure PPR recovers half again as many co-changed symbols as 1-hop expansion (.222 → .336). The gain is largest for tests (.192 → .324). This fits M4's two-hop patterns: tests reach the changed function through the class they instantiate (`parent>caller`) or through a shared callee (`callee>caller`). PPR's mass flows along all of those paths at once. Typed BFS ranks them by a product of weights that favours callee chains, which T1 rewards and T2 does not. **The T1 and T2 rankings of PPR vs BFS are opposite.** That is the second time in two milestones that T1 and T2 disagree, and the reason to tune graph parameters on history, not on static-analysis ground truth.
2. **The graph alone still loses to text by a wide margin.** P reaches .336 against A's .617. Its packed precision is 3.5%, below the lexical channel's 6%, because multi-hop neighbourhoods are large.
3. **Flat RRF fusion makes things worse.** Every fused multi-hop variant is below EA, and EA is only level with A. A 40-item multi-hop list gives RRF twice as many structural items to interleave with lexical ones, and multi-hop items that also match lexically get two RRF contributions and jump ahead of single-channel hits. The same mechanism lowers T1 deps R@10 for EA_ppr (.939 → .725).
4. **Latency is within budget.** α = 0.1, ε = 1e-4 adds about 10–20 ms per query on T2 (P 46 ms) and about 8 ms on T1 (deps p50: E 16 ms, E_ppr 24 ms). That is inside design §5.7's 50 ms target for the structural stage.

**What follows.** The structural signal is not useless. P finds a third of the co-changed symbols with no text at all, but RRF cannot weigh it against the lexical channel. Combining them needs per-candidate evidence (PPR mass, hop distance, relation type, BM25F score) in a learned ranker: M9. M5 delivers the PPR score as one of those features.
