## What was built

- **T2 v1.0.** 300 queries from the 50 most recent qualifying commits of six pinned repositories (httpx, starlette, flask, click, requests, attrs), with 1,122 judged symbols. A query is the diff of the commit's anchor file (its largest modified non-test file) plus the commit message. The judged symbols are the ones the same commit changed in its *other* files. The base snapshot is the parent commit. The dataset card lists the filters and the biases; qrels are also written in TREC format.
- **Leakage control.** The co-change index for a query sees only commits strictly older than the query commit.
- **Co-change channel.** `CoChangeIndex` mines file-level evolutionary coupling (Gall et al. 1998; Zimmermann et al. 2004): support, and confidence = support / n(a). Commits touching more than 20 code files are ignored (tangled changes, Herzig & Zeller 2013), and a partner needs support ≥ 2. The channel offers the top 5 coupled files per changed file (confidence ≥ 0.1) and ranks the symbols inside each file by lexical similarity to the change.
- **Overlay.** When the change is structural at the AST level, the changed files are re-extracted at HEAD and re-resolved against the rest of the snapshot, so the structural channel sees calls and symbols that exist only after the change.
- **S2 on T2.** The hot-fixed v1 pipeline with a BM25 first stage, replayed from git, indexes every parent snapshot. v1 names symbols by bare name, so its items are matched leniently (in v1's favour).
- **Diagnostics** (`eval.t2_analysis`): for every judged symbol, its graph distance from the seeds, the relation path, and its rank in each channel; RRF displacement per query.
- **CI.** A `t2-gate` job clones the six repositories at their pins (cached) and gates `eval/results/t2-v2` exactly like T1.

Tests: overlay, co-change index and channel, the cross-file-only structural option, the analysis BFS and the v1 key mapping. 34 backend and 28 eval tests pass.

## The headline, stated plainly

**On ground truth that static analysis did not produce, the graph adds nothing measurable to lexical retrieval, and it hurts early ranking.** A alone has the best R@10, nDCG@10 and MRR. E+A matches A's recall within budget (+.013, not significant) and loses .063 R@10 (p_holm .011). This reverses the T1 picture (M3: E+A deps R@10 .939 vs A .410), as the circularity warning in M3 anticipated. Co-change fused as a third channel lowers recall within budget (−.076, p_holm 4e-4). The overlay has no measurable effect.

The next section explains why. Most of the gap comes from what T2 can and cannot reward, and part of it comes from fixable design choices.

## Why: diagnostics

All numbers come from [`analysis.json`](../../../eval/results/t2-v2/analysis.json) (`python -m eval.t2_analysis`).

### 1. A one-hop graph can reach only a quarter of what changes together

Graph distance from the seeds to each judged symbol, over the union of the base and overlay graphs (the best case for any graph method). Edges are undirected: calls/references, overrides, inheritance, tests, containment. Imports are excluded.

| Distance | all (1,122) | code (373) | tests (749) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 hop | **26.4%** | 26.0% | 26.6% |
| 2 hops | 27.7% | 39.4% | 21.9% |
| 3–4 hops | 34.7% | 24.9% | 39.7% |
| unreachable | 11.1% | 9.7% | 11.9% |
| beyond 2 hops (`relation_missing`, §6.4) | **45.9%** | 34.6% | 51.5% |

E expands one hop, so its ceiling on T2 is about .26. It reaches .22. **Within its reach, E is not worse than text:** of the judged symbols one hop away, E puts 66% into the context and A 60% (tests: E 71%, A 54%; code: E 56%, A 71%).

### 2. What T2 rewards are callers, not callees (the dataset bias, quantified)

At one hop the judged symbols are **callers in 256 cases and callees in 31** (plus 7 tests edges and 2 overrides). A commit changes the code that *uses* a changed function (callers, and above all tests: 190 of the 256), not the code the author only *read*. E's query plan does the opposite for the most common change type: a body change ranks callees first and halves callers (M3 §"What was built"). On T1 that is right by construction. On T2 the callees it ranks first are unjudged, not wrong. The dataset card records this as bias 1. Only T3 can say which of the two the reviewer needs.

The two-hop patterns are informative for M5: `callee>caller` (127, other users of something the seed calls), `parent>caller` (72, mostly tests instantiating the changed method's class), `caller>callee` (30) and `caller>caller` (27).

### 3. Noise accounting: no channel is precise on T2

| System | packed items (mean) | judged relevant | packed precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| A, lexical items | 29.7 | 1.78 | 6.0% |
| E, structural items | 11.0 | 0.65 | 5.9% (8.3% excluding anchor-file items) |
| C, co-change items | 24.2 | 0.98 | 4.0% |

T1 suggested graph items were far more precise than lexical ones (54–57% vs 5–8%). On T2 they are not. But **28% of E's packed items are in the anchor file**, which T2 never judges (bias 2), and another 31% are in test files. Both are *lower bounds*: the pooled judgment subset of design §6.2 would tell how many unjudged items are relevant, and it does not exist yet.

### 4. Why E+A loses early ranking: RRF displacement

RRF gives the top structural candidate the same score as the top lexical candidate. EA's top 10 contains on average 4.85 items not in A's top 10, of which 1.91 are structural-only, and only 0.23 of those intruders are judged relevant. In 24% of queries EA pushes at least one relevant item out of A's top 10 (mean 0.42 per query). The 40-candidate cut also bites harder after fusion: 17% of judged symbols in EA's fused list rank beyond 40, against 11% in A's lexical list.

### 5. Lexical candidate generation is the bottleneck of the strongest channel

A deep lexical search (1,000 per seed, no score floor) finds 97% of the judged symbols; the lexical channel as configured (50 per seed, score floor .15) generates 66%. Failure stages for A: 556 in context, 97 generated but packed out, 469 never generated. All six systems have the full judged set in their base snapshot (`entity_missing` = 0). M6 (BM25F field weights and query weighting) targets this directly.

### 6. Co-change: real signal, wrong place in the pipeline

C alone reaches .357 with no lexical or structural input, so file coupling carries signal. But it adds almost nothing new: co-change generates 29% of the judged symbols and only **0.9%** that neither the lexical nor the structural channel generates. As a third RRF channel it contributes 18 packed items per query at 4% precision and pushes lexical items out of the budget. That is why EAC loses .076 recall. Co-change belongs in the reranker as a feature (coupling confidence of the candidate's file), which is how design §5.4 lists it. That is M9.

### 7. The overlay costs latency and buys nothing here

Δ R@budget −.004 (n.s.). 94% of T2 changes are structural at the AST level, but the case where the base graph knows nothing about a seed is rarer: only 18% of queries have a seed *added* by the change. For the others, the base graph's edges of the changed symbol are mostly still right. Building the overlay rebuilds the whole graph (+145 ms mean); M7's incremental update would make it cheap. It stays on because it is correct (a test proves it surfaces calls that exist only at HEAD), but T2 cannot show its value.

## Diagnostic ablations

These three systems were run to separate the effects above. They are diagnostics, not tuned systems, so nothing below is selected on T2.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E_noplan: query plan off (uniform relation weights) | E | −.008 (n.s.) | +.014 (.76) | +.004 (1.0) | The deps-first plan is **not** what holds E back |
| E_xfile: structural restricted to other files | E | +.007 (.008) | **+.032 (4e-4)** | **+.034 (4e-4)** | Removing items T2 cannot judge moves judged ones up. Mostly bias 2, not evidence that same-file context is useless |
| EA_noplan | EA | +.003 (n.s.) | +.013 (.007) | +.001 (1.0) | Small; EA_noplan still trails A on R@10 (−.049, p_holm .059) and nDCG@10 (−.049, p_holm .013) |

None of the three changes the headline. The plan is not the problem, and the anchor-file effect explains only a fraction of the gap to A.

## What this changes

1. **Graph-vs-text claims.** None of the T1 graph advantages survive on history ground truth. The honest statement after M4: lexical retrieval is the strongest single channel; one-hop graph expansion is competitive only on what it can reach, and that is a quarter of what changes together.
2. **T2's blind spot.** T2 cannot reward context that is read but not changed. The graph's characteristic contribution (callees, contracts, same-file helpers) falls exactly there. That is a reason to build T3 (M10), not a reason to declare the graph useful.
3. **Fixable design issues the data points at:**
   - flat RRF lets low-precision structural items take top ranks → learned fusion with relation and distance features (M9), or channel weights fitted with leave-one-repo-out CV, never on the full T2;
   - co-change as a reranking feature, not a channel (M9);
   - lexical candidate depth and field weights (M6);
   - the multi-hop patterns that matter on real changes are siblings via a shared callee and callers of the changed method's class, not callee chains (M5 must be evaluated on T2, not only on T1's `deps-2hop`).
4. **S2 on T2.** The hot-fixed v1 pipeline recovers 4.5% of what changes together (A: 61.7%). 13.6 of its 15.3 context items per query come from the changed file itself. That is consistent with the Phase 1 audit (budget starvation by the changed file's own nodes, finding W2) and confirms on real history that any v2 configuration is a large step up from what production serves.
