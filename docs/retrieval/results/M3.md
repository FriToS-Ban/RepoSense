## What was built

- **Structural channel (`expand`).** Typed one-hop expansion from every seed over the v2 graph. Relations and base weights:

  | Relation | Weight |
  |---|---|
  | callee on a changed line | 1.0 |
  | caller | 0.9 |
  | override / overridden | 0.8 |
  | other callee | 0.6 |
  | tests / tested | 0.5 |
  | importer | 0.5 |
  | enclosing class | 0.4 |

  The seed's change-type plan keeps the planned relations at full weight and halves the rest, so a signature change ranks callers ahead of callees. Score = weight × plan multiplier × calibrated edge confidence. Unlike the lexical channel, **same-file symbols are eligible**, because the diff does not show the callees next to a change.
- **Fusion:** Reciprocal Rank Fusion (Cormack et al. 2009, k = 60), with channel weights configurable.
- **Packing labels** carry the structural relation, e.g. `callee on changed line`, `uses place_order · line 4`, `overridden`.
- **Noise accounting** in `eval.run_v2`: for each channel, how many packed items it contributed and how many of those are judged relevant.

3 new tests (31 backend tests total), covering plan-driven ordering, callee on a changed line vs other callee, RRF arithmetic and same-file eligibility.

## Results on T1

| System | deps R@10 | deps nDCG@10 | deps MRR | deps R@budget | impact R@10 | impact nDCG@10 | impact R@budget | items packed | p95 latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (lexical, M2) | .410 | .214 | .181 | .599 | .747 | .458 | .899 | ~31 | 67 ms |
| **E** (graph) | .854 | .553 | .450 | **1.000** | .878 | .585 | .996 | ~6 | 59 ms |
| **E+A** (RRF) | **.939** | **.589** | .447 | **1.000** | **.952** | **.636** | .995 | ~32 | 68 ms |

Paired tests (Holm-adjusted):
- **E+A vs A:** every metric improves in both categories, p_holm ≤ 8e-4.
- **E+A vs E:** recall within budget is identical; fusion improves *early ranking* for deps (R@10 +.085, p_holm 8e-4; nDCG@10 +.036, p_holm .027). For impact the improvements are not significant after correction (p_holm .08).

### Read this before quoting the table

**T1 cannot decide graph vs text.** Its ground truth is produced by the frozen reference resolver, and v2 recovers 100% of that resolver's pairs (M1). Perfect recall for E is therefore *expected by construction*: it shows that the pipeline delivers the structural relations through linking, expansion and packing without loss, not that graph retrieval is better for reviews. That comparison needs ground truth not derived from static analysis: T2 (history, M4) and T3 (end-to-end, M10).

What T1 does show without circularity:
1. **The pipeline loses nothing between graph and context.** Every judged dependency reachable in one hop reaches the 12k-character context: failure taxonomy 480/480 in context. v1 lost 60% of an oracle's candidates in packing.
2. **Ranking:** relations on changed lines and plan-driven priorities put the judged items early. E's MRR is .45, against A's .18.
3. **Noise profile.** In E+A about 27 of 32 packed items come from the lexical channel, and only 5–8% of them are judged relevant; graph items are 54–57% judged relevant. The other ~45% of graph items are *unjudged by construction*: callers in a deps query, enclosing classes, overrides. T1 cannot tell whether they or the lexical filler help a reviewer. The packed-precision gap is the main question for T2 and T3.
4. **Multi-hop is untouched.** 2-hop recall is E .04 and E+A .15; the latter comes from lexical matches. This is M5's (PPR) target.
5. **E leaves the budget mostly unused** (~6 items, mostly in full). Whether to fill it, and with what, is again an empirical question for T2.
